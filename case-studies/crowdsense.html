Turning camera frames into crowd context.

A computer-vision pipeline connecting person detection with occupancy, risk indicators, historical analytics, and reports.

The problem

A person detector produces observations, but monitoring a space needs more context: how many people are present, how occupancy changes, whether the camera is still connected, and what happened earlier. CrowdSense connects the inference output to an operational dashboard rather than leaving it in a detector window.

The approach

YOLOv8 and OpenCV handle person detection, FastAPI exposes monitoring and analysis endpoints, and a JavaScript dashboard presents counts and trends with Chart.js. SQLite retains historical records, while video upload analysis and PDF reporting provide review paths beyond the live camera feed.

  1. Read camera frames or video
  2. Detect people
  3. Calculate occupancy and risk
  4. Publish current state and history
  5. Inspect analytics and reports

Key decisions

Connect inference to a usable monitoring surface

The detector, API, and dashboard are separate parts of the workflow. Detection output becomes counts, occupancy indicators, alerts, and historical records that can be inspected outside the camera window.

The tradeoff. Separating those components makes deployment more flexible, but requires consistent data contracts and clear failure states.

Treat freshness as part of the data

The state layer writes a timestamped detector heartbeat and atomically replaces the state file. Missing, invalid, or stale state is returned as camera offline rather than continuing to present an old observation as current.

The tradeoff. A freshness threshold can classify a slow or interrupted detector as offline, so it needs to match the actual update rate.

Keep live monitoring and uploaded-video review

A camera detector supports live observations, while the upload analyzer provides a second path for examining recorded footage. History and report generation make previous observations available for review.

The tradeoff. Video inference has material memory and compute requirements; hosting the dashboard alone does not provide the inference capacity.

Where it stands

The repository includes live person detection, crowd counting, risk classification, occupancy monitoring, a history-backed dashboard, video analysis, and PDF reporting. It demonstrates the path from computer-vision output to an inspectable monitoring application.

Current scope

Detection quality depends on camera angle, lighting, occlusion, and the model. Risk labels are application indicators, not validated crowd-safety predictions. Live inference and deployment availability were not independently verified for this case study.

A useful next evaluation would compare counts with labeled footage across different crowd densities and test how the dashboard handles delayed or interrupted detector updates.
