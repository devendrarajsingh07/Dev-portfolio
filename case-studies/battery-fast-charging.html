AI Battery Fast Charging / Case study07 — 09

Exploring the tradeoffs in faster charging.

A reinforcement-learning experiment balancing charge progress with modeled voltage, temperature, and battery aging.

Explore the story View source
Project
Reinforcement learning · Simulation
Engineering focus
Continuous control, reward design, and model boundaries
Technology
Python · Soft Actor-Critic · Gymnasium · PyTorch · Streamlit · Plotly

The problem

Increasing charging current can speed up charge progress while also increasing heat and stress. A controller therefore has to balance competing objectives rather than maximize current alone. This project explores that balance in a simulated lithium-ion cell, where the state and reward can be inspected step by step.

The approach

A Gymnasium environment combines a simplified Single Particle Model with thermal and state-of-health calculations. A Soft Actor-Critic agent selects a continuous charging current from observations of charge, voltage, temperature, and health. Training scripts, saved model weights, plots, and a Streamlit dashboard support experimentation.

  1. Observe simulated cell state
  2. Choose a charging current
  3. Advance the cell model
  4. Apply reward and cutoffs
  5. Inspect the charging trajectory

Key decisions

Use continuous control

The environment exposes charging current as a bounded continuous action rather than a small set of fixed switches. Soft Actor-Critic provides the policy implementation through Stable-Baselines3.

The tradeoff. A policy can exploit inaccuracies in its simulator, so smooth control alone does not establish a useful physical charging strategy.

Encode competing objectives in the reward

The reward encourages charge progress while penalizing voltage overshoot, elevated temperature, health degradation, and high current when already warm. Episode termination conditions provide additional modeled boundaries.

The tradeoff. The chosen weights and thresholds determine what the policy learns; they require evaluation rather than being evidence of a physical safety guarantee.

Use a simplified model for iteration

The training environment uses a simplified cell model and thermal update, with a separate direct PyBaMM environment available as a reference. This keeps the training workflow focused on a tractable simulation.

The tradeoff. Simplification omits electrochemical behavior that matters outside the simulated setting.

Where it stands

The repository contains a trainable SAC agent, a continuous charging environment, pretrained weights, and a Streamlit inspection interface. The result is a control-policy experiment that exposes charge, thermal, voltage, and aging objectives together.

Current scope

This is simulation research, not a charger controller validated on hardware. No measured reduction in real charging time, temperature, or cell degradation is claimed. Full electrochemical coupling, aging datasets, and hardware-in-the-loop work are listed as future directions.

The next experiment would compare the learned policy with fixed-current and conventional charging baselines across matched initial states before considering any physical-cell evaluation.
